1. OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The CFM56-7B is a high-bypass turbofan engine developed through a joint venture
between CFM International (a partnership of GE Aviation and Safran Aircraft
Engines). It is the exclusive powerplant for the Boeing 737 Next Generation
family of commercial aircraft and represents one of the most widely used jet
engines in commercial aviation history.

Engine Designation:      CFM56-7B
Engine Type:             Dual-spool, high-bypass turbofan
Bypass Ratio:            5.1:1
Maximum Thrust:          27,300 lbf (sea level static, ISA conditions)
Overall Pressure Ratio:  32.7:1
Fan Pressure Ratio:      1.65
Turbine Inlet Temperature (max): 1,415°C (2,579°F)
Engine Dry Weight:       5,765 lb (2,615 kg)
Length:                  98.7 in (2,507 mm)
Fan Diameter:            61.0 in (1,549 mm)
Certification:           FAA, EASA airworthy

The dual-spool design separates the low-pressure (LP) and high-pressure (HP)
spools, allowing each to rotate at its optimal speed. The LP spool consists of
the fan and low-pressure compressor (LPC) connected to the low-pressure turbine
(LPT). The HP spool consists of the high-pressure compressor (HPC) connected to
the high-pressure turbine (HPT). This architecture improves thermodynamic
efficiency across a wider range of operating conditions.

The high bypass ratio of 5.1:1 means that for every unit of air passing through
the engine core, 5.1 units bypass the core through the fan duct. This results
in superior fuel efficiency and reduced noise compared to turbojet designs.

2. KEY COMPONENTS
================================================================================

2.1 Fan Assembly
    - Number of blades: 38
    - Material: Titanium alloy (Ti-6Al-4V)
    - Fan diameter: 61 inches (1,549 mm)
    - Fan tip speed (max): 1,350 ft/s
    - Blade attachment: Dovetail root with snubber
    - Each blade is individually replaceable and tracked by serial number
    - Fan case incorporates abradable honeycomb liner for blade tip clearance
      control and containment capability per FAR 33.94

2.2 Low-Pressure Compressor (LPC)
    - Number of stages: 4
    - Also referred to as the booster
    - Overall pressure ratio (LPC): 1.7:1
    - Rotor material: Titanium alloy
    - Stator material: Aluminum alloy and titanium
    - Variable stator vanes: None (fixed geometry)
    - Connected directly to fan via LP shaft

2.3 High-Pressure Compressor (HPC)
    - Number of stages: 9
    - Overall pressure ratio (HPC): 14.7:1
    - Rotor material: Titanium (stages 1-6), Nickel alloy (stages 7-9)
    - Stator material: Steel and nickel alloy
    - Variable inlet guide vanes (VIGV): Present on stage 1
    - Variable stator vanes (VSV): Present on stages 2-5
    - Bleed ports: Stage 5 (customer bleed) and stage 9 (high-pressure bleed)

2.4 Combustion Chamber
    - Type: Annular combustion chamber (single annular combustor, SAC)
    - Number of fuel nozzles: 20 (dual-orifice)
    - Liner material: Hastelloy X with thermal barrier coating
    - Igniter location: 2 igniters at 4 o'clock and 8 o'clock positions
    - Combustion efficiency: >99.5%
    - Emissions: CAEP/8 compliant

2.5 High-Pressure Turbine (HPT)
    - Number of stages: 2
    - Blade material: Single-crystal nickel superalloy with TBC coating
    - Blade cooling: Internal convective and film cooling with trailing edge ejection
    - Nozzle guide vane (NGV) material: DS nickel superalloy
    - Turbine inlet temperature (max): 1,415°C
    - HPT stages drive the HP compressor directly via HP shaft

2.6 Low-Pressure Turbine (LPT)
    - Number of stages: 4
    - Blade material: Conventional cast nickel superalloy
    - Blade cooling: Limited (stage 1 only)
    - LPT drives fan and LPC via LP shaft
    - Pressure ratio across LPT: approximately 5:1

2.7 Exhaust Nozzle
    - Type: Fixed area convergent nozzle (primary)
    - Thrust reverser: Cascade-type with blocker doors
    - Thrust reverser actuators: Hydraulically operated
    - Exhaust gas temperature (EGT) thermocouple ring: 8 probes

3. SENSOR PARAMETERS AND NORMAL OPERATING RANGES
================================================================================

All sensor readings should be monitored continuously during operation. Deviations
outside normal ranges shall trigger maintenance investigation per Section 4.

T24  | Compressor Inlet Temperature
     | Normal range:  15°C to 35°C (59°F to 95°F)
     | WARNING threshold: >40°C or <10°C
     | Sensor type: Thermocouple, Pt100 RTD
     | Location: LPC inlet, 2 o'clock position
     | Notes: Influenced by ambient conditions; correct for ISA deviation

T30  | High-Pressure Compressor Outlet Temperature
     | Normal range:  450°C to 620°C (842°F to 1,148°F)
     | WARNING threshold: >640°C
     | CRITICAL threshold: >680°C (triggers FADEC alert)
     | Sensor type: Type K thermocouple
     | Location: HPC exit diffuser, 3 o'clock and 9 o'clock positions

T50  | Low-Pressure Turbine Outlet Temperature (EGT proxy)
     | Normal range:  400°C to 550°C (752°F to 1,022°F)
     | WARNING threshold: >570°C
     | CRITICAL threshold: >620°C
     | Sensor type: Type K thermocouple harness (8 probes)
     | Location: LPT exit plane

Ps30 | High-Pressure Compressor Static Pressure
     | Normal range:  200 psi to 350 psi
     | WARNING threshold: <190 psi or >365 psi
     | Sensor type: Piezoelectric pressure transducer
     | Location: HPC exit casing bleed port

phi  | Fuel-to-Air Ratio (equivalence ratio)
     | Normal range:  0.025 to 0.035
     | Calculated from fuel flow (Wf) and corrected airflow (W2)
     | Abnormal values indicate combustion or fuel metering anomalies

NRf  | Fan Speed Ratio (corrected)
     | Normal range:  0.95 to 1.05 (ratio to design point)
     | WARNING threshold: <0.90 or >1.08
     | Sensor type: Magnetic speed sensor on LP shaft
     | Units: Dimensionless (corrected for inlet temperature)

BPR  | Bypass Ratio
     | Normal range:  4.8 to 5.4
     | Calculated from fan mass flow and core mass flow measurements
     | Deviation may indicate core or fan damage

htBleed | High-Throttle Bleed (normalized bleed valve position)
        | Normal range: 0 to 1 (0 = fully closed, 1 = fully open)
        | Customer bleed extractions reduce core pressure ratio
        | Monitor for stuck valve conditions

W31  | HPT Coolant Flow Rate
     | Normal range:  10 lb/s to 20 lb/s
     | WARNING threshold: <8 lb/s
     | Sensor type: Coriolis mass flow meter
     | Location: HPT cooling air supply manifold

W32  | LPT Coolant Flow Rate
     | Normal range:  8 lb/s to 16 lb/s
     | WARNING threshold: <6 lb/s
     | Sensor type: Differential pressure flow element
     | Location: LPT cooling air duct

================================================================================

CODE | FAULT DESCRIPTION              | SEVERITY | RECOMMENDED ACTION                        | RESP TIME
-----|--------------------------------|----------|-------------------------------------------|----------
F001 | Fan blade erosion              | MEDIUM   | Inspect/replace fan blades                | 24h
F002 | Compressor stall               | HIGH     | Check inlet conditions, perform borescope | 8h
F003 | Combustion instability         | HIGH     | Inspect fuel nozzles, clean combustor     | 12h
F004 | HPT blade creep                | CRITICAL | Immediate borescope, replace HPT blades   | 4h
F005 | LPT blade damage               | HIGH     | Borescope inspection, replace if needed   | 8h
F006 | Bearing failure - LP shaft     | CRITICAL | Immediate engine removal                  | 2h
F007 | Bearing failure - HP shaft     | CRITICAL | Immediate engine removal                  | 2h
F008 | Vibration anomaly              | MEDIUM   | Check balance, bearing wear               | 16h
F009 | Oil pressure low               | HIGH     | Inspect oil system, check pump            | 6h
F010 | Oil temperature high           | HIGH     | Check oil cooler, reduce power            | 4h
F011 | Fuel flow sensor fault         | MEDIUM   | Calibrate/replace sensor                  | 12h
F012 | T24 sensor fault               | LOW      | Replace temperature sensor                | 24h
F013 | T30 overtemperature            | CRITICAL | Reduce thrust, borescope HPC              | 2h
F014 | Ps30 out of range              | MEDIUM   | Check pressure taps, recalibrate          | 12h
F015 | Exhaust gas overtemperature    | CRITICAL | Reduce power immediately, inspect         | 1h
F016 | Fan blade FOD impact           | HIGH     | Borescope fan, assess damage              | 6h
F017 | Bleed valve stuck              | MEDIUM   | Clean/replace bleed valve                 | 8h
F018 | Ignition system fault          | HIGH     | Replace igniter plugs                     | 6h
F019 | Thrust reverser fault          | HIGH     | Inspect actuators, hydraulics             | 8h
F020 | Oil leak detected              | MEDIUM   | Locate and repair seal                    | 12h

FAULT CODE DETAILS:

F001 - Fan Blade Erosion
  Trigger: Leading edge erosion detected via visual inspection or performance
  degradation (thrust loss >2%). Erosion caused by ingestion of sand, dust,
  or water droplets at high velocity.
  Action: Blend repair per limits in AMM Chapter 72-21. If erosion exceeds
  blend limits, replace blade and re-balance fan assembly.

F002 - Compressor Stall
  Trigger: Sudden pressure fluctuation in Ps30, accompanied by loud bang or
  flame from inlet. FADEC records surge event.
  Action: Borescope all HPC stages. Check inlet total pressure distortion.
  Verify VSV rigging. Inspect for FOD damage.

F006 - Bearing Failure (LP Shaft)
  Trigger: High vibration (N1 channel), rising oil temperature, metal particles
  in oil filter, or ECAM/EICAS bearing temperature alert.
  Action: Immediate engine shutdown. Remove engine from aircraft. Replace LP
  shaft bearing set (forward and aft). Inspect shaft for scoring.

F015 - Exhaust Gas Overtemperature
  Trigger: T50 exceeds 620°C for more than 5 seconds. Immediate FADEC derate.
  Action: Reduce thrust to idle. Land at nearest suitable airport. After
  landing, perform EGT over-temperature inspection per AMM 77-10. Borescope
  HPT and LPT before return to service.

5. MAINTENANCE INTERVALS
================================================================================

5.1 500-Cycle Check (Approximately every 6 weeks for short-haul operations)
    - Visual inspection of fan blades (leading and trailing edges)
    - Check fan blade dovetail retention
    - Inspect fan cowl latches and seals
    - Engine oil change (use only approved MIL-PRF-23699 turbine oil)
    - Oil filter element replacement
    - Oil system chip detector check
    - Check thrust reverser actuation system
    - Inspect igniter plug condition and gap (replace if gap >0.080 in)
    - Review FADEC fault history download

5.2 1000-Cycle Check (Approximately every 3 months)
    - All items in 500-cycle check, plus:
    - Full borescope inspection of all LPC stages (4 stages)
    - Full borescope inspection of all HPC stages (9 stages)
    - Borescope inspection of combustion chamber (all 20 fuel nozzle ports)
    - Borescope inspection of HPT stage 1 and stage 2 blades
    - Borescope inspection of LPT stage 1 blades
    - LP and HP bearing vibration trending review (ACARS data)
    - Fuel nozzle flow check (remove 4 nozzles for bench test, rotate selection)
    - VSV actuator rigging check
    - Customer bleed valve cycling test
    - Engine mount and pylon inspection

5.3 5000-Cycle Check (Full Engine Overhaul - Engine Shop Visit)
    - Engine removal from aircraft
    - Complete disassembly and inspection of all modules
    - Fan blade replacement (full set, or as determined by inspection)
    - All bearing replacement (LP forward, LP aft, HP forward, HP aft)
    - HPT blade and nozzle guide vane replacement
    - LPT blade replacement (stages as determined)
    - All internal seals and labyrinth seals replacement
    - Combustion liner replacement or refurbishment
    - Fuel nozzle overhaul (all 20 nozzles)
    - Compressor wash and cleaning
    - Non-destructive testing (NDT) of critical rotating components
    - Dimensional inspection of shafts, discs, and cases
    - Engine reassembly to new-part tolerances
    - Test cell run and acceptance testing
    - Re-certification to airworthy standard

6. BEARING REPLACEMENT PROCEDURE
================================================================================

Step 1: Engine Preparation and Safety
  Lock out / tag out engine per maintenance safety protocol. Drain oil system
  completely. Allow engine to cool to ambient temperature (minimum 2 hours
  after last operation). Install engine stand adapter and secure engine on
  maintenance stand. Install inlet and exhaust covers.

Step 2: Module Disassembly Sequence
  Remove fan cowl panels. Disconnect all electrical connectors, oil lines, and
  fuel lines. Remove fan assembly (front fan frame and LP compressor module).
  Separate HP compressor module from combustion case. Access bearing compartments
  by removing front and rear bearing support housings.

Step 3: Bearing Removal
  Heat bearing housing to 100°C using induction heater to ease bearing removal.
  Use bearing puller tool (P/N: 157A1050G01) to extract bearing from shaft.
  Do not apply force to bearing balls or rollers. Inspect shaft journal for
  scoring, fretting, or corrosion. Measure shaft diameter; replace shaft if
  outside tolerance of ±0.0005 in.

Step 4: New Bearing Installation
  Clean bearing bore and shaft journal with approved solvent. Apply thin coat
  of engine oil to bearing bore. Cool new bearing in dry ice for 30 minutes.
  Install bearing using hydraulic press and bearing installation tool. Verify
  bearing is fully seated against shoulder. Torque bearing retaining nut to
  specified value (HP bearing: 350 ft-lb, LP bearing: 220 ft-lb).

Step 5: Reassembly and Oil System Priming
  Reassemble all modules in reverse order of disassembly. Install new O-rings
  and seals throughout. Reconnect oil lines, fuel lines, and electrical
  connectors. Perform oil system pre-fill and prime. Run engine at ground idle
  for 5 minutes; check for oil leaks and unusual vibration.

Step 6: Post-Replacement Testing and Documentation
  Perform full engine run per acceptance test procedure. Record N1, N2, EGT,
  oil pressure, oil temperature, and vibration levels. Vibration must be
  below 0.3 in/s at all power settings. Complete maintenance records and
  update engine log book. Return engine to serviceable status.

7. BLADE INSPECTION CHECKLIST
================================================================================

Item 1: Leading Edge Condition
  Inspect fan and compressor blade leading edges for nicks, dents, gouges,
  and erosion. Use blade inspection template to measure nick depth. Nicks
  exceeding 0.040 in depth require blend repair or blade replacement.

Item 2: Trailing Edge Condition
  Check trailing edges for cracks, curling, or delamination. Any cracks at
  trailing edge are cause for immediate removal. Trailing edge deformation
  greater than 5 degrees requires replacement.

Item 3: Airfoil Surface Condition
  Inspect pressure and suction surfaces for corrosion pitting, scratches,
  and erosion damage. Measure surface roughness where suspected. Corrosion
  pitting deeper than 0.010 in requires repair or replacement.

Item 4: Root Attachment and Dovetail
  Remove each fan blade and inspect dovetail for fretting wear, cracks, and
  corrosion. Inspect platform shoulders for wear. Dovetail fretting exceeding
  0.005 in wear depth requires replacement.

Item 5: Tip Clearance
  Measure blade tip clearance using feeler gauge at 4 circumferential locations
  (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°). Normal tip clearance: 0.030-0.060 in. Clearance
  exceeding 0.080 in indicates excessive casing wear or blade thinning.

Item 6: Blade Twist and Camber
  Use optical measurement system to check blade aerodynamic profile at 25%,
  50%, and 75% span stations. Blade twist deviation greater than 0.5 degree
  from nominal is cause for removal and further evaluation.

Item 7: Foreign Object Damage Assessment
  Following any bird strike or hard landing event, perform detailed FOD
  inspection using borescope. Document all damage with photographs. Assess
  whether blade is within blend repair limits or requires replacement.

Item 8: Vibration and Balance Check
  After any blade replacement, perform fan assembly balance check. Residual
  imbalance must not exceed 0.5 oz-in per plane. Use trim balance weights as
  needed. Document balance correction weight and position in engine records.

8. VIBRATION LIMITS
================================================================================

Vibration measurements are taken at the fan case (N1 channel) and core case
(N2 channel) using accelerometers. Units are velocity in inches per second
(in/s) peak, measured at operating speed.

NORMAL:   Less than 0.5 in/s
  Engine is operating within acceptable vibration limits. Continue normal
  operation. Record vibration trend data for analysis.

WARNING:  0.5 to 1.0 in/s
  Elevated vibration detected. Reduce power setting to reduce vibration if
  possible. Schedule borescope inspection within next 25 flight hours.
  Check fan blade balance. Investigate bearing condition via oil analysis.
  Increased monitoring frequency required; take readings every 10 flight hours.

CRITICAL: Greater than 1.0 in/s
  Unacceptable vibration level. Immediate engine shutdown required. Do not
  attempt to continue operation. Remove engine from aircraft. Perform full
  borescope inspection of all rotating components. Inspect all bearings for
  failure. Inspect fan blades for loss of a blade segment or FOD. Engine must
  not return to service until vibration cause is identified and corrected,
  and post-repair vibration verified below 0.5 in/s.

Note: Transient vibration spikes above 1.0 in/s during start or shutdown are
acceptable, provided steady-state vibration is within NORMAL limits. Document
any exceedances in the engine health monitoring system.
